# Gregory (1981) Research: Current Relevance for the US Healthcare and Employee Benefits Landscape

## Executive Summary

Gregory's (1981) research on healthcare benefits risk analysis remains **highly relevant** for the US, but requires adaptation to address the significant changes in the healthcare and employee benefits landscape over the past 43 years. The core methodological insights about risk-aware decision-making are more important than ever, but the specific applications and parameters need updating.

## Current Relevance Assessment

### ✅ **HIGHLY RELEVANT: Core Methodological Framework**

**Gregory's Key Contributions Still Apply:**
1. **Risk-aware decision making** - More critical than ever given healthcare cost volatility
2. **Expected utility theory** - Provides rigorous framework for complex benefits decisions
3. **Funding mechanism comparison** - Self-funded vs. fully insured decisions remain central
4. **Risk sensitivity analysis** - Essential for understanding organizational risk tolerance
5. **Stochastic dominance** - Framework for comparing uncertain outcomes

### ⚠️ **NEEDS UPDATING: Market Context and Parameters**

**Significant Changes Since 1981:**
1. **Healthcare cost inflation** - Costs have increased dramatically
2. **Regulatory environment** - ACA, ERISA, and other regulations have changed the landscape
3. **Technology and data** - Advanced analytics and real-time data availability
4. **Risk management tools** - More sophisticated stop-loss and captive insurance options
5. **Employee expectations** - Broader benefits beyond traditional healthcare

## Current US Healthcare Benefits Landscape (2024)

### **Self-Funded vs. Fully Insured Market Share**

**Current Statistics:**
- **61% of covered workers** are in self-funded plans (Kaiser Family Foundation)
- **39% of covered workers** are in fully insured plans
- **Trend:** Continued growth in self-funding, especially among mid-size employers

**Key Drivers of Self-Funding Growth:**
- Cost control and transparency
- Regulatory flexibility
- Customization of benefits
- Risk management sophistication

### **Healthcare Cost Trends (2024)**

**Current Cost Pressures:**
- **2024 premium increases:** 8.5-9.1% projected
- **2025 projections:** 9.2% increase expected
- **Employer strategies:** Increased employee contributions, high-performance networks, clinical case management

**Risk Management Evolution:**
- **Stop-loss insurance** - More sophisticated and customized
- **Captive insurance** - Growing adoption by larger employers
- **Reference-based pricing** - Alternative to traditional networks
- **Direct contracting** - Bypassing traditional insurance intermediaries

### **2. Funding Mechanism Comparison - EVOLVED BUT RELEVANT**

**Gregory's Original Comparison:**

- Risk smoothing vs. cost reduction trade-offs

**Current Applications:**
- **Fully insured** - Predictable costs, higher premiums, limited customization
- **Self-funded with stop-loss** - Lower base costs, higher risk, more control
- **Partially self-funded** - Hybrid approaches with risk sharing
- **Captive insurance** - Self-insurance with formal risk management

- Risk aversion parameter calculations
- Stochastic dominance analysis

**Required Updates:**
- **Cost parameters** - Update to current healthcare cost levels
- **Variability estimates** - Reflect current cost volatility
- **Risk aversion ranges** - Update based on current organizational risk tolerance
